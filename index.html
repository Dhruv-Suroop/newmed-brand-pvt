Single source of truth for the
NewMed Skills identity.

The idea underneath.

NewMed Skills moves qualified clinicians across borders — and replaces an anxious, low-trust process with something calm, specific and sequential.

What we do

A fragmented journey, made one clear path.

NewMed Skills sources, assesses, trains, licenses and deploys workforce-ready healthcare professionals for hospitals across major geographies. One platform manages the entire journey, from first assessment to first shift, sourcing clinical talent from India, Asia and Africa and deploying licensed, verified professionals to the UK, GCC, Europe and North America.

Training is our differentiator. We don't just screen candidates — we assess exactly where they stand, then curate and teach them the specific skills they need to reach their career goals and meet international clinical standards.

Two people rely on us. The candidate — a nurse or doctor ready to work abroad. And the hospital — a team that needs qualified people without six months of unscreened CVs.

Positioning

Trust is demonstrated,
not claimed.

Healthcare staffing is a low-trust category. Competitors sound like either body-shop salesmen — hype, urgency, promises — or enterprise software — sterile, jargon, “solutions.” Both try to manufacture trust, and both fail. The opening neither can occupy is clinical calm: the voice that doesn't need to raise itself to be believed. We write and design the way a good mentor speaks — someone who has walked this a hundred times and still remembers what it felt like not to know. They tell you plainly what to expect, never oversell, and stay calm because they have seen it work out. The warmth is in being on your side, not in softening the facts.

The journey

Six stages, one direction.

The shape of the brand

The identity has one direction built into it. From exam to licence. From licence to placement. From one country to the next.

Everything moves from › to

An N built entirely from arrows — a path, not a badge. It carries the ambition and optimism of moving forward, and the momentum that turns intent into real progress.

A letter that points forward

The counters and terminals are cut as arrowheads. The letter is made of the movement it describes.

Personality

Four attributes, always on.

01CertainSettled, factual, unhurried. Every claim carries a number or a mechanism, so confidence comes from substance, never volume — most of all when the stakes are high. We inform someone competent enough to draw their own conclusion.
02PlainClear, concrete, direct. Short sentences, one idea at a time. Written as if translating for a capable new colleague who doesn't know the acronyms yet.
03WarmSteady, human, on the reader's side. Warmth lives in naming the real stakes, fees, visas, arriving alone, not in soft words.
04GuidingDirectional and sequential. From and to constructions, numbered steps, action verbs: assess, train, license, deploy, support.

The mark.

An N constructed from arrows. Forward motion made from the letter itself. It carries the ambition and optimism of moving forward, and also creating momentum to make real progress. Give it room, keep it in its approved colours, and never redraw it.

Icons, rounded and calm.

Icons carry small pieces of meaning through the journey — a step, a status, a service. They stay quiet and consistent, echoing the soft geometry of the arrow so they never feel bolted on from elsewhere.

How our icons look

One simple, consistent style throughout. When you need an icon that is not shown here, draw it to these same four rules so the set always reads as one family.

Single colour

One colour per icon — Deep Brown by default, or cream when it sits on a dark or orange ground. Never multi-colour, never a gradient.

Thick strokes

Weighted, confident lines that hold up at small sizes. Nothing thin, faint or wiry.

No sharp corners

Rounded ends and joins throughout, matching the arrow's soft caps. Calm, never spiky.

Simplified and clean

Reduce each idea to its clearest form. No clutter, no ornament, no unnecessary detail.

Gradients

Gradients are always organic, soft and flowing: layered off-centre washes fading into cream, never hard-edged circles and never a colour from outside the palette. They carry ambient warmth behind a hero statement, and they never compete with the words on top of them.

The organic gradientThe master wash. Yellow and orange bleed off the edges and resolve into cream, with no visible centre and no hard edge anywhere.

How a radial is built

Each radial runs from a single palette colour at full strength out to cream, across a long falloff. The construction below marks the two endpoints. Keep the falloff generous: a short one produces a visible ring, which reads as a hard edge.

One typeface, calm and confident.

The whole brand is set in Mona Sans — a humanist grotesk that stays clear and plainly legible across sizes. It is pared back, allowing the words stand out as the loudest thing on the page. It works in unison with the arrow to carry the promise.

Mona Sans

Modern and open, it is clearly legible to an audience for whom English is often a second language. It holds its character across dense body text and big headings. Headings are set tight and in bolder weights, allowing them to speak confidently. Body is set in regular weight, and spaced to allow lightness and clarity in reading.

Setting rules

A few tips keep the type calm and on-brand.

Sentence case

Headings and buttons are sentence case. ALL-CAPS is only for small eyebrow labels.

No orphans

Never leave one word alone on a line in a headline. Use irregular line widths, or make the orphan the arrow phrase.

One idea per line

Headlines under eight words; body sentences under twenty. Every benefit answers “how?” in the same breath.

Numbers carry confidence

Use figures when a number carries meaning — a 1:3 ratio, four steps, day one.

Highlighting text

The key words of a headline sit inside the arrow, allowing emphasis. Highlight the phrase that carries the movement or the promise, never filler and never the negative half of a sentence. Type and graphic work together, reading as a complete sentence.

Two words or more

The arrow marks the phrase that carries the movement or the promise — never a single word, never a whole sentence.

Make it the last line

End the headline on the highlight so no word is left orphaned. The phrase in the arrow completes the sentence.

One arrow per headline

Pull the reader to a single, expressive phrase. Two competing highlights cancel each other out, creating no emphasis.

Maintain contrast

The phrase in the arrow always stays legible. Cream text on orange (gradient or solid) arrow. Deep brown text on yellow arrow.

Gradient fill by default

Default is the NewMed Orange to Yellow gradient. Flat orange, yellow or deep brown are alternates when the gradient cannot be used.

How the arrow is built

The arrow is designed to perfectly frame your text. It is exactly twice as tall as the uppercase letters, providing generous and even padding on all sides. This ensures the text has room to breathe and never touches the edges. The rounded tail and pointed head always maintain their exact shape and proportions, no matter how long the text is.
